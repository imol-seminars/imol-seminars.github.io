Abstract

This overview of the free energy principle offers an account of embodied exchange with the world that associates neuronal operations with actively inferring the causes of our sensations. Its agenda is to link formal mathematical descriptions of dynamical systems to a description of perception in terms of beliefs and goals.

The argument has two parts: first, it calls on the lawful dynamics of weakly mixing ergodic systems, from a single-cell organism to a human brain. These dynamics suggest that internal states can be interpreted as modelling or predicting the external causes of sensory fluctuations. Second, the only tenable beliefs I can entertain about myself are that I exist. This is equivalent to believing that the world, and the way it is sampled, will resolve uncertainty about the causes of sensations.

The talk will consider implications for functional anatomy, predictive coding and hierarchical architectures, concluding with simulations of active inference and the epistemic behaviour that emerges.

About the speaker

Karl J. Friston, MBBS, MA, MRCPsych, MAE, FMedSci, FRBS, FRS, is Professor at the Queen Square Institute of Neurology, University College London, and Honorary Consultant at The National Hospital for Neurology and Neurosurgery.

He is a theoretical neuroscientist and authority on brain imaging. He invented statistical parametric mapping (SPM), voxel-based morphometry (VBM), and dynamic causal modelling (DCM). These contributions were motivated by schizophrenia research and theoretical studies of value-learning, formulated as the dysconnection hypothesis of schizophrenia.

His mathematical contributions include Variational Laplace and generalized filtering for hierarchical Bayesian model inversion. Friston currently works on models of functional integration in the human brain and on the principles that underlie neuronal interactions. His main contribution to theoretical neurobiology is the free-energy principle for action and perception, also known through the active inference framework.

Friston has received numerous distinctions, including the Young Investigators Award in Human Brain Mapping, the Minerva Golden Brain Award, the Weldon Memorial Prize and Medal, the Charles Branch Award, and the Glass Brain Award. He is a Fellow of the Royal Society, the Academy of Medical Sciences, and the Royal Society of Biology, and a member of EMBO and Academia Europaea. He holds honorary doctorates from the Universities of York, Zurich, and Radboud University.
