Abstract

This overview of the free energy principle offers an account of embodied exchange with the world that associates neuronal operations with actively inferring the causes of our sensations. Its agenda is to link formal mathematical descriptions of dynamical systems to a description of perception in terms of beliefs and goals.

The argument has two parts: first, it calls on the lawful dynamics of weakly mixing ergodic systems, from a single-cell organism to a human brain. These dynamics suggest that internal states can be interpreted as modelling or predicting the external causes of sensory fluctuations. Second, the only tenable beliefs I can entertain about myself are that I exist. This is equivalent to believing that the world, and the way it is sampled, will resolve uncertainty about the causes of sensations.

The talk will consider implications for functional anatomy, predictive coding and hierarchical architectures, concluding with simulations of active inference and the epistemic behaviour that emerges.

About the speaker

Karl J. Friston is a theoretical neuroscientist at University College London. He is known for major contributions to brain imaging, including statistical parametric mapping, voxel-based morphometry and dynamic causal modelling, and for his work on the free-energy principle and active inference.
